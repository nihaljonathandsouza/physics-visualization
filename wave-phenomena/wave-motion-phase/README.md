# Wave Motion, Phase & Superposition

A Manim-based visualization exploring the mathematical description of travelling waves, their physical parameters, phase and velocity, followed by wave superposition and interference.

## Concepts Visualized

- Travelling waves
- Amplitude
- Wavelength
- Wave number
- Frequency
- Angular frequency
- Phase
- Phase velocity
- Superposition
- Constructive interference
- Destructive interference
- Wave packets
- Beats

## Overview

The animation begins with a travelling wave and gradually introduces the physical quantities that describe its behaviour.

The wave is explored through its amplitude, wavelength, wave number, frequency, angular frequency, and phase.

The animation then develops the relationship between wave number, angular frequency, and the velocity of wave propagation.

It subsequently introduces the principle of superposition by combining waves and examining the resulting waveform.

Different phase and frequency relationships are used to demonstrate constructive and destructive interference, followed by more complex wave behaviour including wave packets and beats.

## Mathematics

A travelling wave is represented as:

$$
E(x,t) = A\cos(kx-\omega t+\phi)
$$

where $A$ is the amplitude, $k$ is the wave number, $\omega$ is the angular frequency, and $\phi$ is the phase.

The wave number is related to wavelength by:

$$
k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
$$

and angular frequency is related to frequency by:

$$
\omega = 2\pi f
$$

The phase velocity is:

$$
v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}
$$

The principle of superposition gives:

$$
E_R = E_1 + E_2
$$

The animation then demonstrates how combining waves with different parameters produces more complex wave behaviour.

## Teaching Focus

The visualization builds from the mathematical description of a single travelling wave toward phase, wave propagation, superposition, and interference.

The animation is intended to connect the mathematical parameters in the wave equation with their physical meaning and observable behaviour.

## Implementation

**Software:** Python, Manim Community Edition, NumPy

The animation uses dynamically generated mathematical waveforms, vector representations, parameter changes, and animated transformations to visualize the behaviour of travelling and superposed waves.
